# Shared Knowledge Review Rubric

Automation establishes structure and resolvable contracts. A reviewer remains
responsible for the judgments below.

## Reader Intent

- Does one primary question justify the selected profile?
- Does the guide stay within that question and route adjacent needs elsewhere?

## Technical Claims And Evidence

- Are claims correct, and does each cited location support the adjacent claim?
- Are observations, evidence, inference, recommendations, and normative source
  requirements distinguishable?
- Does the guide explain what its evidence cannot establish?

## Depth And Version Scope

- Is the guide the smallest sufficient treatment for its profile?
- Are major-line distinctions materially useful rather than incidental?
- Does provenance remain separate from reader applicability?

## Safety And Realism

- Are mutation, cost, privilege, network, and external-service effects clear?
- Are examples safe, internally consistent, adaptable, and non-publishing by
  default?

## Coherence

- Is terminology consistent with the foundations?
- Does the guide link instead of duplicating another guide's substantive
  guidance?
- Are summaries, questions, relationships, sources, status, and review date in
  the catalog accurate?
